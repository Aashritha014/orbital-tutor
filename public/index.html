Quantum numbers

SymbolNameAllowed valuesTells you
nPrincipal1, 2, 3, …Shell and size/energy of the orbital
lAngular momentum0 to n − 1 (s=0, p=1, d=2, f=3)Subshell and orbital shape
mℓMagnetic−l to +lOrientation; gives 2l + 1 orbitals per subshell
mₛSpin+½ or −½Direction of the electron’s spin

So s has 1 orbital (2 e⁻), p has 3 (6 e⁻), d has 5 (10 e⁻), f has 7 (14 e⁻).

1. Aufbau principle

Electrons fill the lowest-energy subshell available first.

Order: 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p 5s 4d 5p 6s 4f 5d 6p 7s 5f 6d 7p

2. Pauli exclusion

An orbital holds at most two electrons, and they must have opposite spins. No two electrons share all four quantum numbers.

3. Hund’s rule

Within a subshell, electrons occupy separate orbitals with parallel spins before any pairing begins. Nitrogen’s 2p³ is ↑ ↑ ↑, not ↑↓ ↑ _.

Exceptions and why they happen

ElementExpectedActualReason
Cr (24)[Ar] 3d⁴ 4s²[Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹Half-filled d subshell: each d orbital has one electron, which is extra stable.
Cu (29)[Ar] 3d⁹ 4s²[Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹A completely filled d subshell is extra stable.
Mo (42)[Kr] 4d⁴ 5s²[Kr] 4d⁵ 5s¹Same half-filled d reasoning as Cr.
Pd (46)[Kr] 4d⁸ 5s²[Kr] 4d¹⁰Fully filled 4d; the 5s electrons drop in.
Ag (47)[Kr] 4d⁹ 5s²[Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s¹Same as Cu.
Au (79)[Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d⁹ 6s²[Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d¹⁰ 6s¹Same as Cu.

Why: the 3d and 4s energies are very close, and half-filled or full subshells gain extra stability from electron exchange and symmetry. So one electron moves from s to d. Other exceptions (such as La, Ce, Gd, Pt, and several actinides) are highlighted on the Periodic Table tab.
